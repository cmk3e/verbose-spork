How preflop ranges, 3-bets and postflop commitment change from deep stacks to push/fold

The inverse value seesaw

DEEP STACKSSuited connectors, small pairs, suited wheel aces
SHALLOW STACKSBig offsuit cards: KJo, KTo, ATo, A9o, QJo
As stacks get shorter, big offsuit cards gain value: they make top pairs you can get all in with. As stacks get deeper, suited connectors and small pairs gain value: they make the straights, flushes and sets that win big pots.

One exception at the very short end: once you're shoving, small pairs and weak aces gain value again for their all-in equity and blockers.

Why suited connectors and wheel aces?

If you 3-bet only JJ+ and AK, a 5-6-7 flop misses you completely and opponents can attack it. Bluffs like 65s, 76s and A5s/A4s make straights, two pairs and wheels on those boards, so you're much harder to exploit on low, connected flops.

Your 80bb BTN vs LJ chart agrees: it 3-bets A2s 95%, 65s 75%, 87s 60% and T9s 50% of the time as bluffs.

100bb: QJs, JTs, 65s and 76s are 3-bets. Deep, a 3-bet pot is big and still has room to play, so you want hands that make straights and flushes and cover low and middle boards. K9s–K6s just call: they make second-best flushes and weak top pairs, which are costly in big pots.
40bb: the lojack can 4-bet jam over your 3-bet, so the bluffs become K9s, K7s and K6s: the king blocks KK and AK, and they fold easily to a jam. QJs, JTs, 65s and 76s now just call: in position at 40bb they play well postflop and hate being jammed off their equity.

The math

More players in the pot means a bigger pot for the same stacks, so a lower SPR, even at 150bb.

The shift

Strong top pairs and overpairs gain value, but so do opponents' two pairs and sets: with more players, someone has hit more often. Bluff less.

Example

Deep, with two early limpers, over-limp 44 behind them rather than isolate: it hates most flops and wants a cheap set. Don't open-limp from early position.

Effective stacks and opponent tendencies

The effective stack rule

100bb vs 25bb = 25bb

The effective stack is the smaller stack in the hand: it's the most you can win or lose. With 100bb against a 25bb player, play a 25bb strategy against them. Against another 100bb player, you're deep again. Use the stacks of the players who can enter the pot, not the table average.

The pattern

  • Deep, you 3-bet small or call. At 80bb nothing in this table re-jams.
  • At 30bb, the later the opener, the more you re-jam. A button or cutoff opens a wide range that can't call a jam, so 66, ATo and AQo shove against them.
  • Against an early opener, mostly call. UTG's tight range calls a jam with the hands that crush you, so 66, ATo and even AQo usually just call.
  • Hands that play well after the flop but can't call a jam (A5s, KJo) call at every position.
  • Overall vs a button open: small 3-bets drop from 16% to 6% of hands; re-jams rise from 0% to 11%.

Shove or fold. Each orbit costs about 2.5bb, so shove before you're too short to make anyone fold.

Normally you call 9bb to win 21bb, so 43% equity is enough. On the bubble, busting costs you a sure min-cash and doubling up doesn't double your prize, so you need roughly 12% more.

More examples

  • QQ vs AK with two big stacks: QQ is only about 55%, but with both players cashing comfortably you'd need around 70%. Fold.
  • Big stack vs a short stack's jam: losing barely dents you, so call about as wide as normal.
  • Medium stack vs another medium stack: the most dangerous all-in on the bubble. Avoid it without a premium.
  • As the big stack, open and shove wider: medium stacks have to fold too much.
  • Your charts ignore payouts: on the bubble, call tighter than the charts.
