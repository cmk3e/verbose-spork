The static fallacy vs the dynamic strategy

The static fallacy

🔒

Weaker players use one opening range at every stack depth, ignoring what happens after the flop.

The dynamic strategy

Good players keep roughly the same number of hands but change which hands as the effective stack shrinks. These are your real lojack charts.

The inverse value seesaw

DEEP STACKSSuited connectors, small pairs, suited wheel aces
SHALLOW STACKSBig offsuit cards: KJo, KTo, ATo, A9o, QJo
As stacks get shorter, big offsuit cards gain value: they make top pairs you can get all in with. As stacks get deeper, suited connectors and small pairs gain value: they make the straights, flushes and sets that win big pots.

One exception at the very short end: once you're shoving, small pairs and weak aces gain value again for their all-in equity and blockers.

3-betting: the deep-stack strategy

Stack depth

Webinar example: about 44 value combos and 36 bluff combos, so the range is balanced.

Why suited connectors and wheel aces?

5♠
6♥
7♣

If you 3-bet only JJ+ and AK, a 5-6-7 flop misses you completely and opponents can attack it. Bluffs like 65s, 76s and A5s/A4s make straights, two pairs and wheels on those boards, so you're much harder to exploit on low, connected flops.

Your 80bb BTN vs LJ chart agrees: it 3-bets A2s 95%, 65s 75%, 87s 60% and T9s 50% of the time as bluffs.

The math

More players in the pot means a bigger pot for the same stacks, so a lower SPR, even at 150bb.

The shift

Strong top pairs and overpairs gain value, but so do opponents' two pairs and sets: with more players, someone has hit more often. Bluff less.

Example

Deep, with two early limpers, over-limp 44 behind them rather than isolate: it hates most flops and wants a cheap set. Don't open-limp from early position.

Effective stacks and opponent tendencies

The effective stack rule

100bb vs 25bb = 25bb

The effective stack is the smaller stack in the hand: it's the most you can win or lose. With 100bb against a 25bb player, play a 25bb strategy against them. Against another 100bb player, you're deep again. Use the stacks of the players who can enter the pot, not the table average.

Adjust to opponents

They fold too much to 3-bets3-bet more blocker bluffs
They call 3-bets with weak handsDrop the bluffs; 3-bet more value hands

What changes

  • Open smaller (2–2.3bb). The BTN tightens from 54% (80bb) to 45% (30bb) to 36% (20bb) because the blinds re-jam.
  • Re-jam with blockers and hands that do well all in: Ax, KQ, KJ, pairs.
  • Call jams tighter than you'd jam (the gap concept): you need a hand ahead of their range.
  • Postflop: SPR 3–4.5 makes top pair good kicker a stack-off; bet small and plan to get it in.

Facing a shove

At 12bb the BB calls a BTN shove with about 28% of hands and an SB shove with about 40%.

Under 10bb

Shove or fold. Each orbit costs about 2.5bb, so shove before you're too short to make anyone fold.

How it plays

  • Example: 10bb in the BB vs a 40bb SB shove with any two: you need about 56% (44+, A6s+, A8o+, K6s+, K8o+, Q8s+, JTs), not 43%.
  • Big stack: open, 3-bet and defend wider; medium stacks can't fight back.
  • Medium stack: avoid the big stacks; pressure other medium and short stacks.
  • Short stack: shove, don't min-raise and call off.
  • Your charts are chip EV: tighten calls by the risk premium on the bubble.
