Rules of thumb

  1. Raise small bets, call big ones. Against ⅓ pot the BB check-raises about 20%; against ¾ pot only about 9%. A big bet already gets folds, and it means the bettor holds more strong hands.
  2. Out of position, raise; in position, call. Flush draws check-raise 29% out of position but raise only 6% in position. In position you see every turn and river for the price of a call.
  3. Raise with the top and with strong draws; call with the middle. Two pair or better raises 67%, open-enders 30%, but top pair with a weak kicker raises only 14% and weak pairs 12%. Middle hands beat bluffs but hate a re-raise.
  4. Raise more on boards that help the caller. Low and paired boards, where the big blind holds more sets, two pairs and straights.
  5. Shorter stacks: raise-or-fold with top pair. At 20bb top pair with a good kicker check-raises 65% (vs 50% at 50bb): a raise is close to all in, so get it in now.
  6. On the turn, raise less and bluff less. With one card to come, draws are worth less: the BB check-raises the turn about half as often as the flop, mostly with two pair or better.

Shallow, one-pair hands turn into raises: a check-raise puts you close to all in, so you take the money now. Draws and gutshots raise less, because a bluff-raise can't fold anyone out with so little behind. Sets and two pair raise less too: the stack goes in later anyway, so they let the button keep betting.

With so little behind, top pair and good draws raise much more (top pair, good kicker 44%). The stack goes in on the flop, so raising gets your equity in before a scary turn. Sets and two pair often just call to keep bluffs in.
